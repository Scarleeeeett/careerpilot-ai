# Industry research framework

## 1. Industry boundary

Answer in order: primary industry, subsector, product form, application scenario, adjacent industries, and why common alternative labels are incomplete. Define the market by customer problem and transaction, not technology alone.

## 2. Demand and customers

Identify payer, direct user, operator, beneficiary, regulator, and procurement decision-maker separately. Explain the old workflow, bottleneck, changed workflow, and remaining manual work.

## 3. Value chain

For each layer, state participants, inputs, outputs, bargaining power, and money flow:

```text
Upstream components/data/infrastructure
→ focal company integration and delivery
→ channel or operating customer
→ end user or service recipient
```

Include software, data, compliance, insurance, implementation, and after-sales when material.

## 4. Product and technology

Describe the complete product: hardware or data inputs, core models or rules, orchestration, human operations, output, failure handling, and feedback loop. State capability boundaries and operating conditions.

## 5. Market and competition

Analyze market size and definition, adoption stage, player types, concentration, entry barriers, substitutes, switching cost, and likely consolidation. Segment competitors before ranking them.

## 6. Business model and economics

For each model, identify payer, charging unit, contract length, included items, recurring revenue, variable costs, and prerequisites.

```text
Unit revenue - variable unit cost = contribution margin
Lifecycle cost / completed tasks = unit cost
Initial investment / annual net saving = static payback period
```

For TCO include acquisition, software, deployment, energy, insurance, maintenance, operations, human intervention, downtime, and residual value when relevant. Label assumptions.

## 7. Policy and risks

Consider access, licensing, safety, liability, privacy, cybersecurity, localization, standards, customer concentration, supplier dependence, reliability, and scalability. Translate each rule into product, delivery, or commercial implications.

## 8. Cases and trends

For each case include participants, old workflow, new workflow, disclosed result, source, and limitation. Separate observed change from forecast.
